The principle

Metal + Making charge + Stones Rounded to the nearest ₹100

How coloured stones are applied

Catalogue designs and custom designs

Catalogue designs use their exact weight from your sheet, per metal.

The online customizer uses an indicative 3 g setting weight, because a piece designed from scratch has no weight until it is specified. This is stated on screen, and the final weight is confirmed at consultation.

Fixed and indicative

Fixed — making charges, purity, and the per-carat stone rates. All taken directly from your sheet.

Indicative — the total. It moves with the daily metal rate, and the final stone is chosen and confirmed at consultation. Every price on the site is labelled accordingly.

Keeping rates current

The 24K gold rate and the silver rate are the only two numbers that need maintaining. Update them in one place and every price across the site recalculates — catalogue and customizer together. Everything else stays fixed until your price sheet changes.

Needs a decision

Natural diamonds are not priced online

We have hidden natural diamonds from the online customizer for now, and we would like to agree the right approach with you before switching them on.

The reason is straightforward. Your price sheet provides rates for lab-grown and coloured lab-grown diamonds, which is what the site now uses. It does not include a rate card for natural stones. In the earlier build, the natural price was produced by applying a fixed multiple to the lab-grown rate — that gave a number, but not one either of us could stand behind in front of a customer.

Natural diamond pricing also does not behave like lab-grown. Price per carat steps up sharply at the 0.30, 0.50, 1.00 and 1.50 carat thresholds, and varies considerably with shape, colour, clarity and cut. A single per-carat figure, however carefully chosen, will overstate small stones and understate large ones.

Rather than publish a figure we cannot justify, we have taken it off the customizer. Natural diamonds remain fully available and are quoted at consultation against the actual stone, which is the only accurate way to price them.

To bring natural diamonds online, we would need

  1. Your natural diamond rate card, or the basis you price against — your supplier's list, or your own.
  2. A decision on structure: whether to price in carat bands, for example 0.30–0.49, 0.50–0.99 and 1.00–1.49, rather than one flat per-carat rate.
  3. Whether colour and clarity grades should carry separate rates, as they realistically do.

Once we have those, adding natural diamonds to the customizer is a small change. We would rather spend a short conversation getting it right than show a price that has to be corrected at consultation.
